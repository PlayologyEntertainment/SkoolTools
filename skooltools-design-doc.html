01

Vision & principles

The why

SkoolTools is a single page that loads in under a second, holds thirty-plus tiny tools a student actually uses in a school day, and never asks them to sign up, install anything, or hand over their data. Study4Class proved the demand. SkoolTools is the version a student would screenshot and send to a friend.

"School is the user's job. Our job is to disappear into it."

1.1Five principles

  • One page, no install. Everything lives in a single HTML file. Open it on a Chromebook in class, on a phone on the bus, on a borrowed laptop at the library. It works offline once loaded.
  • Tool first, brand second. The student came here to solve a thing. Branding sets the tone but never the obstacle. No splash screens, no nags, no email walls.
  • Local by default. Notes, flashcards, GPAs, checklists — all saved to the device. Nothing leaves the browser unless the student explicitly exports it.
  • Small tools, sharp edges. Each tool does one thing exceptionally. We resist becoming a do-everything dashboard. If a tool can't justify its tile, it goes.
  • Playful, never childish. A sixteen-year-old will not tolerate cartoons. They will tolerate — and quietly enjoy — a site that respects them with good typography, sharp interactions, and a sense of humour.
Why "SkoolTools" and not "Study4Class 2.0"? The new name signals a clean slate: a different aesthetic, a curated toolset, an opinion about what matters. The intentional "Skool" misspelling earns permission to be a little irreverent — which is the entire point of the Playology brand.

Audience & jobs-to-be-done

Who, what for

2.1Primary: the high-schooler

Ages 14–18. Carrying a Chromebook with locked-down extensions. Switching between Google Docs, a calculator app, a class website, and TikTok. Time-poor and attention-fragmented. Cares more about speed of access than feature depth.

  • Job: "I have to turn in something by 11:59pm and I just need the answer to one thing without a five-step funnel."
  • Job: "I'm in class right now and I need a calculator that doesn't get me in trouble."
  • Job: "I'm studying for the SAT/AP exam tomorrow and I need flashcards that don't suck."

2.2Secondary: the college student

Ages 18–24. Personal laptop, multiple monitors, a Notion or Obsidian habit. Less about quick utilities, more about writing support, citation accuracy, GPA tracking across semesters, and exam-week productivity rituals.

  • Job: "I'm three weeks into the semester and need to model whether I can still get a B in this class."
  • Job: "My professor said APA 7. I have a website, a journal article, and a book chapter. Format them correctly in two minutes."
  • Job: "It is 1am, I have 1,200 words to go, and I need a Pomodoro that doesn't preach at me."

2.3Anti-audience

SkoolTools is not for: teachers building gradebooks, parents tracking children, schools needing FERPA-grade data warehousing, or anyone wanting an AI tutor. Those markets are well served. We are deliberately a personal tool for the student.

5.1The twelve new tools, in detail

  • Graphing Calculator. Plot one or more functions; pinch-zoom; tap to read coordinates; auto-find roots and intersections. Replaces both Quadratic Solver and Line Graph.
  • Geometry Solver. Pick a shape (triangle, circle, rectangle, prism, sphere, cylinder, cone), enter what you know, get every derivable property. Replaces Right Triangle and goes much further.
  • Vocabulary Builder. For SAT/ACT/AP prep. Paste a list, get definitions, build a deck. Sits next to Flashcards but specialized.
  • Language Drill. Spanish/French/German verb conjugation and noun-gender drills. The most-requested feature in language-class subreddits.
  • Focus Lock. Fullscreen mode that disables most page navigation, shows only a countdown and current task. Soft-block, escapable, but enough friction to keep a phone-tempted brain on-task.
  • Assignments & Planner. Replaces Study Checklist. Add a class, add an assignment with due date, see what's due today/this week. Calendar export to .ics.
  • Schedule Builder. Weekly grid with periods and rooms. Print to PDF for binder. Color by class.
  • Code Playground. Run JS in a sandboxed iframe; HTML/CSS live preview; Python via Pyodide loaded on demand. For CS class.
  • Currency (inside Unit Converter). Bundled with last-fetched ECB rates; updates when online.
  • Notes upgrade. Cornell, Outline, Q&A, and Free templates in one tool with markdown render + print stylesheet.
  • Citation upgrade. Chicago/Turabian added; journal articles and book chapters as source types; DOI lookup pulls metadata automatically when online.
  • Flashcards upgrade. SM-2 spaced-repetition lite (Again/Hard/Good/Easy) and Anki .csv import for students already invested in Anki decks.

6.4Navigation rules

  • Search-first. A focused search bar at the top of the page accepts fuzzy queries. Typing "perc" surfaces Percentage. Hitting Enter opens the top hit.
  • Pinning. Each tool card has a ☆ in the top-right. Pinned tools appear in a "Pinned" row above the shelves.
  • Recents. The last five tools used appear next to Pinned. Cleared by clearing browser storage.
  • Keyboard. / focuses search · Esc closes panel · ? opens shortcuts overlay · G then C jumps to Calculator (Vim-style).
  • Deep links. Every tool has a hash URL students can bookmark or paste in chat.
07

Interaction patterns

How it feels

7.1Inputs

  • One large primary action per panel. Calculate, Solve, Convert. Solid Ink button, never accent — the accent is reserved for state and identity.
  • Auto-compute when safe. Word counter, unit converter, base converter recompute on input. Expensive operations (graphing, stats) require the button.
  • Paste from spreadsheet. Anywhere a list of numbers is expected, accept comma, space, tab, and newline equally.

7.2Outputs

  • Big primary result. Display font, monospaced numerals, large enough to read across a classroom.
  • Show work. Toggle in math tools that reveals intermediate steps — for the student who actually has to write them down.
  • Copy & export. Every result has a one-tap copy. Notes export to .md, schedules to .ics, decks to .csv.

7.3State & persistence

  • localStorage namespace. All keys prefixed st: (e.g. st:flashcards:decks).
  • Backup & restore. Settings has a "Download my data" button (single JSON) and a matching "Restore from file." Critical for Chromebook students whose browser data gets wiped.
  • Per-tool reset. Each tool has its own clear/reset; no global hidden state mystery.
  • Versioned migrations. A small migration runner converts old keys when the schema changes between releases.

7.4Motion

One animated moment per interaction, never more. Panel opens with a 180ms fade-and-rise; results pulse the accent border once when computed; the Pomodoro tick is a subtle 1px shift, not a bouncing icon. Reduced-motion is fully honored — all transitions collapse to opacity changes when prefers-reduced-motion is set.

08

Accessibility & performance

For all students, in any classroom

8.1Accessibility budget

  • WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for all text and UI states (verified per palette pair).
  • Full keyboard reachability; visible focus ring on every interactive element.
  • Semantic HTML — real headings, real buttons, real labels, real aria-live regions for computed results.
  • Screen-reader announcements when a tool panel opens and when a result is produced.
  • Honor prefers-reduced-motion, prefers-color-scheme, and forced-colors mode.
  • Text resizes up to 200% without overflow or clipping.
  • Touch targets ≥ 44×44px on mobile; calculator keys ≥ 56×56px.
  • Dyslexia-friendly mode in Settings — switches body to a high-readability sans (e.g. Atkinson Hyperlegible), increases line-height to 1.8, widens letter-spacing.

8.2Performance budget

<1sFirst contentful paint, 3G
<200kbInitial HTML+CSS+JS, gzipped
0Network calls before interactive
100%Offline after first load

Heavy dependencies (KaTeX for formulas, Pyodide for Python, charting library for graphing) are lazy-loaded the first time their host tool is opened, with a loading state. Students who never open the Code Playground never download Pyodide.

09

Technical architecture

How it's built

SkoolTools ships as a single .html file. No build step required to run it. A simple build step (concatenation + minification) exists to assemble it from per-tool source files for maintainability.

Stack

  • Vanilla HTML, CSS, JavaScript. No framework. ES modules consolidated at build time. Zero runtime dependencies for the shell.
  • Variable CSS. Custom properties for theming; [data-theme="dark"] overrides on the root.
  • Tool registry. A single JS array describes every tool — id, name, shelf, icon, deep link, init function. Adding a tool is one entry plus one module.
  • Service worker. Optional install for offline; the page itself is fully usable from cache after first load even without SW.

Lazy dependencies

  • KaTeX — Formula Reference, Graphing Calculator, Geometry Solver, Physics Solver.
  • Chart drawing — Graphing Calculator only (custom <canvas>, no heavy library).
  • Pyodide — Code Playground · Python tab only, deferred until tab is selected.
  • jsPDF — Print/export-to-PDF (Notes, Schedule, Essay Planner).

9.1Tool module contract

// Every tool exports the same shape.
export const tool = {
  id: 'calculator',
  name: 'Calculator',
  shelf: 'numbers',
  icon: '🧮',
  description: 'Scientific, with history & ANS.',
  keywords: ['math','calc','scientific'],
  mount(rootEl, ctx) {
    // ctx provides: storage, theme, modals, kbd
    rootEl.innerHTML = template();
    wireEvents(rootEl, ctx);
    return {
      unmount() { /* clean listeners */ },
      onShortcut(key) { /* optional */ }
    };
  }
};

9.2Storage contract

  • Read. ctx.storage.get('flashcards:decks', defaultValue)
  • Write. ctx.storage.set('flashcards:decks', value) (auto-debounced 300ms)
  • Migrate. ctx.storage.migrate(version, fn)
  • Quota guard. If storage fills, the shell shows a toast and offers export+clear, never silently drops data.
10

Trust, safety, privacy

What we promise students
"No account. No tracker. No upsell. Your notes never leave your laptop unless you tell them to."
  • No accounts. Period. The original site had a magic-link sign-in for cloud sync; we are dropping it. Cross-device sync, if ever needed, will use end-to-end encrypted export/import codes — never an account.
  • No third-party scripts. No Google Analytics, no Hotjar, no ad networks. If we want usage metrics, we add a one-line, no-cookie, server-side counter that records nothing identifiable.
  • No "Discreet play" or game-cloaking features. The original site had an About:Blank mode and tab-cloak intended to hide the site from teachers. SkoolTools is a study tool; we will not ship features whose primary purpose is to deceive supervising adults.
  • Academic-integrity stance. No paraphrase tool, no AI essay generator, no "homework auto-solver" that just produces answers without showing work. Every math tool can show steps so the student can learn the work, not skip it.
  • Content safety. Sample texts (typing test, vocabulary, language drill) are reviewed for age-appropriateness for a 14-year-old reader.

Success metrics

How we'll know
3+Tools used per student per visit
40%Returning weekly users by month 3
<2sMedian time-to-first-tool-open

Because we collect no personal data, success metrics are measured by aggregate, privacy-respecting counters (page hits, tool-open counts) and by qualitative signal — feedback form, Reddit/TikTok mentions, teachers linking the site in syllabi.

11.1What we will not optimize for

  • Session length. A student who spends less time on SkoolTools because they got their answer fast is a win.
  • Page views per session. See above.
  • Engagement streaks, badges, gamification. SkoolTools is a hammer, not a habit-tracker. The Pomodoro counter is the only streak-like thing and it resets daily.
